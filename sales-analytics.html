Sales Analytics Dashboard

End-to-end sales analytics on a global, multi-table (star schema) dataset, using Python for data cleaning and an interactive Power BI dashboard to show what drives revenue and profit across products, regions, customers and employees.

Problem statement

A retail and sales organization had its transactional data spread across multiple related tables (customers, products, employees, geography, dates and sales transactions). The raw data had quality issues: duplicate records, missing values, inconsistent formatting, invalid numeric entries and orphaned references. This made it unreliable for direct analysis or reporting.

Without a clean, unified view, the business could not easily answer basic questions: where revenue actually comes from, which products and categories are worth prioritizing, whether discounts are eating into profit margins, and which customers and employees drive the most value.

Goal: turn raw, fragmented sales data into a single, trustworthy source of truth that shows sales and profit trends, top products and categories, regional and channel differences, and the top employees and customers.

About the data

  • A global, relational dataset in a star schema, covering multiple regions, product categories, sales channels, employees and customers.
  • Tables: customers, products, employees, geography, dates and sales transactions.
  • Sales channels: Retail, Online, Partner and Phone.
  • Data issues: duplicate records, missing values, inconsistent formatting, invalid numeric entries and orphaned references.

Steps

  1. Cleaned and validated the raw transactional data in Python (pandas): duplicates, missing values, formatting, invalid numbers and orphaned references.
  2. Modeled the cleaned tables into a star schema in Power BI.
  3. Wrote DAX calculations for sales, orders, profit and the other measures.
  4. Built a 3-page interactive dashboard so leadership, product teams and sales teams can each find what is relevant to them.

The dashboard

Page 1: Overview

Headline numbers (total sales, orders and profit) alongside where the revenue comes from: top categories, top countries and the quarter-over-quarter sales trend. The channel breakdown shows how customers actually buy: Retail, Online, Partner and Phone.

Page 2: Product Performance

Shows what is actually selling. Revenue leaders and profit leaders sit side by side, because a product that sells a lot is not always the one making the most money. Category comparison and units-sold trends complete the product view.

Page 3: Customer and Employee Performance

Shows who drives the business: employee contribution on the selling side, and customer value and loyalty tiers on the buying side. It helps spot where the business is well diversified or too dependent on a small group.

Key findings

  • Clothing is the top-performing category with the highest revenue, followed closely by Accessories and Kitchen.
  • Revenue is spread evenly across countries. No single market dominates, and the top 7 countries fall within a narrow range.
  • Sales channels are almost perfectly balanced, each contributing roughly a quarter of total sales, which shows a well-diversified customer base.
  • Employee performance is mostly consistent, with a few standout performers and no extreme outliers, so dependency risk on any one employee is low.
  • A small segment of customers spends nearly 2x the typical customer, a clear opportunity for targeted retention.
  • Top-performing products span multiple categories, so revenue leadership is not concentrated in one product line.
  • Quarterly sales are volatile, with sharp spikes and dips rather than a steady trend. This points to seasonal demand or inconsistent promotions worth investigating.

Business impact

  • Smarter inventory and marketing focus, by identifying which products and categories drive profit and not just revenue.
  • Lower concentration risk, since sales are well diversified across channels, countries and employees.
  • A targeted customer retention strategy, by surfacing high-value customers for loyalty programs.
  • Better promotional planning, since the quarterly volatility shows where discounting or seasonal strategy needs a closer review.
  • A reliable, self-serve view of performance that replaces manual reporting.
